# Durability and Marathon-Oriented Quality Training

Last reviewed: 2026-08-23

Durability describes resistance to deterioration during or after prolonged exercise. It complements fresh-state VO2max, threshold, and running-economy measurements; it does not replace them.

---

## DUR-001 — Durability is a distinct endurance-performance concept

### Research question

What is durability, and why is it different from fresh-state fitness?

### Supported claim

Durability is the resilience of physiological variables and performance to deterioration during or after prolonged exercise. A runner may have strong fresh-state VO2max, threshold speed, or economy but experience different degrees of decline after sustained work. Protocol intensity, duration, nutrition, environment, and training status affect measured durability.

### Training target

Maintain threshold-related speed, economy, pace, and internal-to-external workload relationship as fatigue accumulates.

### Expected transfer

Less late-session or late-race deterioration, particularly for half-marathon and marathon demands.

### Acute evidence

Prolonged running can reduce threshold-related speed, VO2peak, or economy-related measures and increase HR/RPE drift at a fixed external speed.

### Chronic adaptation evidence

The ability to improve durability is plausible and actively studied, but direct intervention evidence identifying the best training method remains limited.

### Performance evidence

Recent runner studies and large observational marathon data associate smaller/slower deterioration with better marathon performance.

### Programming variables

- duration and relative intensity of prior running;
- nutrition/hydration;
- temperature and humidity;
- terrain and eccentric load;
- fresh-state baseline;
- measurement timing and outcome.

### Population / context

Methodological review across endurance research; runner-specific acute studies in recreational and well-trained runners; mass marathon observations.

### Evidence strength

**Moderate** for durability as a measurable concept and performance correlate; **Low** for an optimal intervention.

### Directness to Odyssey

**Moderate**: relevant to longer-race planning, but most durability protocols exceed Tuesday's main-set duration.

### Limitations

Different studies use different fatiguing protocols and outcomes. Durability is not one single number.

### What this does not prove

- that ordinary cardiac drift directly measures all durability;
- that every long session improves durability;
- that poor late-race performance is caused by one physiological decline;
- that one Tuesday session can replace long-run exposure.

### Sources

`PMID-40150840`, `PMID-41128270`, `PMID-39384626`, `PMID-40421635`, `PMID-35511416`

## DUR-002 — Prolonged running can lower threshold-related speed in both recreational and trained runners

### Research question

How much can prolonged running change key performance markers?

### Supported claim

In 31 recreational runners (15 female), 90 minutes at 90% of first-lactate-threshold speed reduced first-threshold speed by about 5-6% in both sexes. In 18 London Marathon participants, a 90-minute run at lactate-threshold speed reduced VO2peak and lactate-threshold speed; smaller deterioration in threshold speed was associated with faster marathon performance. These are small studies with different protocols.

### Training target

Build the ability to preserve controlled speed and physiological markers as fatigue accumulates.

### Expected transfer

Potentially improved ability to hold sustainable pace later in long races.

### Acute evidence

Direct pre/post deterioration in threshold-related speed, with protocol-dependent changes in VO2peak and other variables.

### Chronic adaptation evidence

Not established by these acute studies.

### Performance evidence

The London Marathon study found an association between smaller threshold-speed decline and faster marathon time. The sample was small and cannot establish causality.

### Programming variables

- intensity and duration of the fatiguing run;
- threshold method;
- sex and training status;
- nutrition and environment;
- treadmill versus outdoor running.

### Population / context

- Recreational runners: n=31, 15 female.
- Marathon participants: n=18, 11 male, mean marathon time about 3:17.

### Evidence strength

**Moderate** for acute deterioration; **Low** for causal training recommendations.

### Directness to Odyssey

**Moderate**.

### Limitations

Both protocols were controlled tests, not normal club sessions. The faster 90-minute threshold protocol is a large load and should not be copied into Odyssey.

### What this does not prove

- that 90 minutes is the required durability stimulus;
- that threshold deterioration should be tested every month;
- that female and male runners have identical durability across all contexts;
- that the association guarantees improved marathon time if durability changes.

### Sources

`PMID-39384626`, `PMID-41128270`

## DUR-003 — Internal/external workload decoupling is informative but not a complete prescription metric

### Research question

Can heart-rate-to-speed drift provide useful durability information?

### Supported claim

In 82,303 recreational marathoners, greater and earlier decoupling of internal workload (relative HR) from external workload (speed relative to estimated CS) was associated with slower marathon performance. This shows meaningful real-world variability and predictive value, but it is observational and affected by pacing, heat, hydration, fueling, and measurement error.

### Training target

Use pace stability, effort drift, and form deterioration as contextual feedback, not as a mandatory automated metric.

### Expected transfer

Better recognition of when a sustained workout no longer represents the intended controlled stimulus.

### Acute evidence

Decoupling generally increased later in the marathon.

### Chronic adaptation evidence

Not tested.

### Performance evidence

Magnitude and onset were associated with marathon performance and improved prediction models in the analyzed dataset.

### Programming variables

- HR measurement quality;
- estimated CS;
- temperature;
- dehydration/fueling;
- hills and wind;
- pacing strategy;
- cardiac and perceptual drift.

### Population / context

82,303 recreational marathoners, including 13,125 female; observational mass dataset.

### Evidence strength

**Moderate** for association; **Low** for causal training prescription.

### Directness to Odyssey

**Low-to-Moderate** because Odyssey does not collect participant HR/Strava data systematically.

### What this does not prove

- that every participant needs HR monitoring;
- that drift is always a fitness problem;
- that a fixed drift threshold should stop a session;
- that Odyssey should add participant surveillance.

### Sources

`PMID-35511416`, `PMID-40150840`

## DUR-004 — Evidence for how to improve running durability remains incomplete

### Supported claim

Durability can be measured reliably under controlled conditions, but direct intervention studies comparing training methods are limited. Current practice must therefore distinguish supported performance associations from coaching inference about how to train the quality.

### Training target

Potentially relevant methods include:

- sustained controlled tempo;
- longer threshold/cruise intervals;
- race-specific work later in a cycle;
- whole-program low-intensity volume and long runs;
- fueling and environmental management.

The list is an **inference from broader endurance evidence**, not a ranked evidence-based prescription for durability.

### Expected transfer

Gradual development of fatigue resistance without turning every Tuesday into a long or marathon-specific session.

### Acute evidence

Different fatiguing protocols produce different durability profiles.

### Chronic adaptation evidence

Insufficient direct running trials to identify the best dose, intensity, or intervention.

### Performance evidence

Durability correlates with marathon performance, but causality and trainability dose remain under study.

### Evidence strength

**Low-to-Moderate**.

### Directness to Odyssey

**Moderate** as a rationale for occasional sustained/longer work; **Low** for exact dose.

### What this does not prove

- that continuous tempo is always better than cruise intervals;
- that Tuesday should simulate late-marathon fatigue;
- that durability is only relevant to marathoners;
- that high fatigue is necessary to train fatigue resistance.

### Sources

`PMID-40150840`, `PMID-40421635`, `PMID-39384626`, `PMID-41128270`

## MAR-001 — Marathon-oriented Tuesday work supports only part of the complete preparation

### Research question

What can a one-hour group Quality Session reasonably contribute to marathon preparation?

### Supported claim

Whole-program evidence links marathon performance with training volume, frequency, long-run variables, low-intensity predominance, threshold/race-specific work, and fatigue resistance. Odyssey can contribute controlled faster running, but cannot reproduce the weekly volume, long runs, individual fueling, or taper management of a complete marathon program.

### Training target

- sustainable threshold speed;
- controlled longer intervals;
- continuous tempo/race-rhythm practice;
- economy and form at meaningful pace;
- maintained aerobic-power/speed qualities where appropriate.

### Expected transfer

Tuesday improves one or more performance determinants while individual training supplies the endurance volume and long-run specificity.

### Acute evidence

Longer controlled work creates sustained aerobic strain and pace-control demand; it does not replicate marathon duration.

### Chronic adaptation evidence

Marathon training patterns are largely supported by observational and practice evidence. Threshold and interval interventions support specific determinants.

### Performance evidence

Training volume, long-run characteristics, and durability measures are associated with marathon performance.

### Programming variables

- time to marathon;
- Tuesday quality duration;
- unknown weekly load;
- pace relative to threshold and marathon goal;
- non-marathon participants;
- heat and recovery.

### Population / context

World-class, sub-elite, and mass-participation marathon evidence; mixed causal certainty.

### Evidence strength

**Moderate** for the broad role; **Low** for exact Odyssey session design.

### Directness to Odyssey

**Moderate**.

### Limitations

Marathon goal pace can be unrealistic or disconnected from current shorter-distance ability. One pace should not be prescribed from aspiration alone.

### What this does not prove

- that every Tuesday after the 1-km TT should be marathon pace;
- that Seoul participants should always do more than non-participants;
- that a community session can manage marathon fatigue or injury risk.

### Sources

`PMID-39616560`, `PMID-31704026`, `PMID-35362850`, `PMID-38695978`, `PMID-40150840`

## MAR-002 — Marathon-specificity should increase selectively, not eliminate varied quality

### Supported claim

Elite/practice and observational evidence describe increased race-specific work nearer the target marathon while threshold and higher-intensity qualities remain present. The exact balance varies, and Odyssey must serve runners not racing Seoul.

### Training target

Increase the relevance of sustained work after the 1-km milestone while retaining occasional aerobic-power, hills, or neuromuscular stimuli when they solve a clear training problem.

### Expected transfer

Marathon participants gain sustainable-speed relevance; non-participants still receive generally useful distance-running quality.

### Evidence type

- Evidence-based: progression toward event specificity and varied whole-program training.
- Inference: which Tuesdays and formats Odyssey selects.

### Evidence strength

**Moderate** for broad progression; **Low** for exact one-session balance.

### Directness to Odyssey

**High** as a planning constraint.

### Sources

`PMID-35362850`, `PMID-35418513`, `PMID-39616560`

## Applied durability boundary for Tuesday

Odyssey may describe a workout as durability-oriented only when the primary purpose includes maintaining pace/quality under sustained controlled fatigue. Avoid using `durability` as a fashionable synonym for any hard or long workout.

Participant-facing language should remain simple, for example:

```text
長めの時間をコントロールして走り、疲れてきてもペースとフォームを保つ練習。
長距離レースの後半でも走りを崩しにくくする土台を狙います。
```

This wording expresses the intended target, not a guaranteed adaptation from one session.
