# Race-Distance Demands and Specificity

Last reviewed: 2026-08-23

This file identifies broad event demands. It does not assign universal energy-system percentages, race paces, or workout formulas.

---

## Cross-event framework

Running performance emerges from interacting determinants:

- maximal aerobic capacity;
- sustainable fraction of aerobic capacity / threshold-related speed;
- critical speed and finite capacity above it;
- running economy;
- anaerobic and sprint/speed qualities;
- durability during prolonged exercise;
- pacing, environment, fueling, and psychology;
- event-specific rhythm and mechanics.

The relative importance changes with event duration and runner profile. A systematic review of non-elite runners found that terminology and predictors vary substantially across 5 km to ultramarathon studies, and most evidence is observational.

## RACE-1K-001 — Approximately 1 km requires substantial aerobic and anaerobic contribution

### Research question

What broad physiological demands should inform training for a 1-km time trial?

### Supported claim

Direct 1000-m intervention and energy-system evidence is limited. In 20 national-standard athletes, estimated aerobic contribution was about 66% for 800 m and 84% for 1500 m, demonstrating that both aerobic and anaerobic systems contribute substantially across the surrounding events. A world-class 800/1500 review describes diverse profiles and combined aerobic-power, speed, strength/power, and speed-endurance demands.

### Training target

Develop complementary qualities:

- aerobic power;
- race-speed familiarity;
- speed endurance;
- speed reserve / neuromuscular quality;
- controlled tolerance of accumulating fatigue.

### Expected transfer

The runner can reach a fast pace, support much of it aerobically, and preserve rhythm as finite above-CS capacity is used.

### Acute evidence

A 1-km effort rapidly combines high oxygen uptake with substantial anaerobic energy release and rising fatigue.

### Chronic adaptation evidence

Indirect from 800/1500 practice, HIIT, sprint, and strength evidence. No single 1-km training formula is established.

### Performance evidence

Direct evidence is sparse; energy-system and elite-practice evidence support a mixed-quality approach.

### Programming variables

- target race duration;
- current speed reserve;
- aerobic power;
- work distance/duration;
- recovery completeness;
- quality volume;
- days to race.

### Population / context

- Energy-system study: 20 highly trained national-standard athletes, small event-specialist subgroups.
- Scientific/practice review: world-class 800/1500 runners.

### Evidence strength

**Moderate** for mixed aerobic/anaerobic demand; **Low** for exact 1000-m prescription.

### Directness to Odyssey

**High** for the 2026-09-19 milestone; **Low-to-Moderate** for exact energy contribution and dose.

### Limitations

1000 m cannot be assigned an arithmetic midpoint between 800 and 1500 m. Athlete profile, race duration, and methodology affect estimates.

### What this does not prove

- that every week should contain 1-km repetitions;
- that 600-m or 300-m repetitions have one mandatory pace;
- that all-out work is required to train anaerobic contribution;
- that one energy-system percentage applies to every runner.

### Sources

`PMID-11194103`, `PMID-34021488`, `PMID-41931241`

## RACE-1K-002 — 1-km specificity should rise without replacing complementary qualities

### Supported claim

Specificity increases as competition approaches, but a mixed-demand event still benefits from aerobic-power and speed qualities. Taper evidence supports reducing load while retaining some intensity near competition.

### Training target

Progress from general aerobic support toward race rhythm, then reduce quality volume while preserving fast movement before the event.

### Expected transfer

The runner recognizes target rhythm and arrives with lower residual fatigue rather than repeatedly rehearsing maximal races.

### Evidence type

- Evidence-based: mixed event demands, progression/specificity, taper load reduction.
- Inference: the exact sequence and spacing of 3-minute, 600-m, and 300-m work.

### Evidence strength

**Moderate** for the sequence principle; **Low** for exact session order.

### Directness to Odyssey

**High**.

### Sources

`PMID-34021488`, `PMID-35362850`, `PMID-37163550`, `PMID-17762369`

## RACE-5K-001 — 3–5 km performance emphasizes high aerobic power, CS, economy, and speed reserve

### Research question

Which broad qualities matter for 3–5 km running?

### Supported claim

Running studies and performance reviews identify strong roles for maximal aerobic capacity/speed, CS, threshold-related speed, and running economy. A finite capacity above CS contributes to high-intensity performance. Speed and anaerobic qualities remain relevant, particularly to shorter race duration, pace changes, and finishing speed.

### Training target

- aerobic power and high-intensity tolerance;
- threshold/CS development;
- economy at fast submaximal speeds;
- race-pace rhythm and speed reserve.

### Expected transfer

A faster sustainable speed and greater ability to operate above that boundary for race-relevant periods.

### Acute evidence

Race pace for many trained runners lies in the severe domain or close to the heavy-severe boundary depending on race duration and performance level.

### Chronic adaptation evidence

HIIT, threshold, and strength interventions can improve relevant determinants. Direct event-specific trials use heterogeneous protocols.

### Performance evidence

CS and aerobic markers are strongly associated with mile/5,000-m performance in reviewed evidence; strength interventions often improve 1.5-10 km TT outcomes.

### Programming variables

- individual race duration;
- pace relative to CS and MAV;
- long versus short intervals;
- race-pace quality volume;
- recovery;
- speed reserve.

### Population / context

Running-specific reviews plus trained runner interventions; some evidence remains observational.

### Evidence strength

**Moderate-to-High** for broad determinants; **Low** for exact event-specific workout formulas.

### Directness to Odyssey

**High** for common quality-session families.

### Limitations

A 15-minute and a 30-minute 5 km have different relative demands. Race pace should not be assigned to one physiological domain for all runners.

### What this does not prove

- that 5-km training is only VO2 intervals;
- that threshold work is not useful;
- that equal-distance repetitions dose all groups equally.

### Sources

`PMID-41931241`, `PMID-35362850`, `PMID-29249083`, `PMID-41900531`

## RACE-10K-001 — 10 km emphasizes high sustainable aerobic speed with retained race-specific intensity

### Research question

What broad qualities should guide 10-km-oriented quality training?

### Supported claim

10-km performance is strongly related to aerobic capacity, threshold/CS-related speed, economy, and the ability to sustain a high fraction of aerobic capacity. CS/D' balance has strong associations with 10,000-m performance in the running scoping review. Speed and aerobic-power work remain useful, but race pace is sustained longer than in 3–5 km events.

### Training target

- threshold/CS speed;
- aerobic power as support for race-pace reserve;
- economy and pace control;
- progressive 10-km-specific duration.

### Expected transfer

The athlete can sustain a faster pace with less relative strain and preserve form/rhythm over a longer severe/heavy-domain duration.

### Acute evidence

Race intensity depends on performance duration and can sit near or above CS. Exact domain location varies by athlete.

### Chronic adaptation evidence

Threshold, HIIT, and strength evidence supports relevant determinants; direct 10-km prescription comparisons are limited.

### Performance evidence

CS/D' and physiological markers are associated with 10-km outcomes. Association does not define the optimal training dose.

### Programming variables

- race duration;
- work duration and cumulative race-pace volume;
- recovery density;
- recent 5/10-km performance;
- fatigue and environment.

### Population / context

Running reviews covering trained and non-elite athletes; heterogeneous performance definitions.

### Evidence strength

**Moderate**.

### Directness to Odyssey

**High**.

### Limitations

Non-elite performance research is often observational and uses inconsistent labels for runner level.

### What this does not prove

- that 10-km pace equals threshold for every runner;
- that every session should be race pace;
- that longer intervals are always superior.

### Sources

`PMID-41931241`, `PMID-41900531`, `PMID-35362850`, `PMID-29249083`

## RACE-HM-001 — Half-marathon performance increases the importance of sustainable speed and durability

### Research question

Which broad qualities become more important as race duration extends to the half marathon?

### Supported claim

Evidence across non-elite running and endurance physiology indicates that threshold/CS-related speed, running economy, training volume, pace control, and resistance to physiological deterioration become increasingly relevant with longer duration. Direct half-marathon intervention evidence is less developed than general endurance evidence.

### Training target

- sustainable threshold-oriented speed;
- longer controlled quality duration;
- economy and pace stability;
- emerging durability/fatigue resistance.

### Expected transfer

The runner can maintain a high submaximal pace with lower drift and less late-race deterioration.

### Acute evidence

Prolonged running can reduce threshold-related speed and change HR/RPE at a given external speed.

### Chronic adaptation evidence

Direct durability-improvement trials are still limited. Whole-program volume and sustained work are plausible contributors but cannot be reduced to one session.

### Performance evidence

Performance-factor evidence is largely observational and distance-specific; training and physiological variables are positively associated with performance.

### Programming variables

- cumulative quality time;
- pace relative to threshold/CS;
- duration without recovery;
- fueling/environment for longer work;
- runner weekly volume outside Odyssey.

### Population / context

Non-elite running systematic review plus durability research in recreational/trained runners.

### Evidence strength

**Moderate** for broad determinants; **Low** for exact Tuesday prescription.

### Directness to Odyssey

**Moderate** because one hour cannot reproduce full half-marathon preparation.

### Limitations

Odyssey does not own long runs or weekly mileage. Durability assessment and training are still emerging areas.

### What this does not prove

- that every half-marathon session must be long continuous tempo;
- that aerobic-power work is unnecessary;
- that one Tuesday creates durability independently of total training.

### Sources

`PMID-41900531`, `PMID-40150840`, `PMID-39384626`, `PMID-29249083`

## RACE-MAR-001 — Marathon demands combine sustainable speed, economy, durability, volume, and environmental/fueling management

### Research question

Which broad factors are supported for marathon performance?

### Supported claim

Marathon evidence supports the importance of whole-program low-intensity volume, threshold and race-specific work, long-run exposure, economy, pace control, and fatigue resistance. Observational analyses associate smaller physiological deterioration/decoupling with faster marathon performance. Most evidence cannot be reproduced by one Tuesday session.

### Training target

Within Odyssey's scope:

- controlled threshold and longer intervals;
- sustained tempo/rhythm;
- occasional marathon-relevant pace control;
- maintaining faster qualities without excessive fatigue.

### Expected transfer

Tuesday can support sustainable speed and quality under controlled duration, complementing—but not replacing—individual weekly volume and long runs.

### Acute evidence

Prolonged exercise can reduce VO2peak, threshold speed, and running-economy-related measures or increase internal/external workload decoupling.

### Chronic adaptation evidence

Whole-program training variables and progression toward race-specific work are supported broadly, but much marathon evidence is observational/practice-derived.

### Performance evidence

Higher training volume, long-run variables, threshold-related speed, and durability measures are associated with performance.

### Programming variables

- whole-week volume;
- long run;
- marathon-pace duration;
- threshold work;
- race-specific progression;
- fueling, heat, terrain, and taper;
- runner experience.

### Population / context

World-class, sub-elite, recreational, and mass-participation marathon data.

### Evidence strength

**Moderate** for broad determinants; **Low** for exact one-hour group prescription.

### Directness to Odyssey

**Low-to-Moderate**.

### Limitations

Associations do not establish causality, and Odyssey does not manage nutrition, long runs, or weekly volume.

### What this does not prove

- that every Tuesday should be marathon pace before Seoul;
- that non-marathoners need a different session every week;
- that a 20-minute tempo is a complete marathon workout.

### Sources

`PMID-39616560`, `PMID-31704026`, `PMID-35362850`, `PMID-41128270`, `PMID-35511416`

## Specificity decision rule

Specificity can increase through any combination of:

- closer race pace;
- more relevant work duration/distance;
- more relevant recovery structure;
- pace judgement over meaningful segments;
- event-specific fatigue while preserving mechanics;
- reduced total load with retained intensity before racing.

Specificity does **not** require repeating the race distance all-out or using race pace every week.
