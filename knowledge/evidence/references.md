# Verified Evidence Reference Registry

Last reviewed: 2026-08-23

This registry stores bibliographic and population/context metadata once. Topic files cite stable source IDs such as `PMID-42237396`.

The `Key use` field states how Odyssey may use the source; it is not a full paper summary.

## Training-intensity distribution and periodization

### PMID-34749417

Campos YAC et al. *Training-intensity Distribution on Middle- and Long-distance Runners: A Systematic Review.* Int J Sports Med. 2022;43(4):305-316. DOI: 10.1055/a-1559-3623.

- Design/population: systematic review of middle- and long-distance runners.
- Key use: whole-program low-intensity predominance; variation between pyramidal and polarized patterns.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34749417/

### PMID-38717713

Oliveira PS et al. *Comparison of Polarized Versus Other Types of Endurance Training Intensity Distribution on Athletes' Endurance Performance: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis.* Sports Med. 2024;54(8):2071-2095. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-024-02034-z.

- Design/population: 17 comparative studies, 437 endurance athletes, interventions ≥4 weeks.
- Key use: small VO2peak advantage for polarized training; no clear advantage for TT, TTE, or VT2/LT2 speed/power.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38717713/

### PMID-29182410

Kenneally M et al. *The Effect of Periodization and Training Intensity Distribution on Middle- and Long-Distance Running Performance: A Systematic Review.* Int J Sports Physiol Perform. 2018;13(9):1114-1121. DOI: 10.1123/ijspp.2017-0327.

- Design/population: systematic review of distance-running interventions/practice.
- Key use: seasonal change in emphasis; limited evidence for a uniquely superior periodization model.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29182410/

### PMID-35418513

Casado A et al. *Training Periodization, Methods, Intensity Distribution, and Volume in Highly Trained and Elite Distance Runners: A Systematic Review.* Int J Sports Physiol Perform. 2022;17(6):820-833. DOI: 10.1123/ijspp.2021-0435.

- Design/population: systematic review of highly trained and elite runners.
- Key use: successful programs combine high low-intensity volume with changing threshold/high-intensity and race-specific work.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35418513/

### PMID-35362850

Haugen T et al. *The Training Characteristics of World-Class Distance Runners: An Integration of Scientific Literature and Results-Proven Practice.* Sports Med Open. 2022;8:46. DOI: 10.1186/s40798-022-00438-7.

- Design/population: scientific/practice review of world-class distance running.
- Key use: progression, race-specificity, and event-dependent training characteristics.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35362850/

### PMID-39616560

Muniz-Pumares D et al. *The Training Intensity Distribution of Marathon Runners Across Performance Levels.* Sports Med. 2025;55(4):1023-1035. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-024-02137-7.

- Design/population: observational analysis of 151,813 marathon performances by 119,452 runners.
- Key use: faster groups accumulated more training mainly through low intensity; pyramidal patterns were common; no causal inference.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39616560/

### PMID-31704026

Doherty C et al. *An evaluation of the training determinants of marathon performance: A meta-analysis with meta-regression.* J Sci Med Sport. 2020;23(2):182-188. DOI: 10.1016/j.jsams.2019.09.013.

- Design/population: meta-analysis/meta-regression of 85 marathon studies.
- Key use: whole-program volume, frequency, and long-run variables are associated with marathon performance.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31704026/

### PMID-38695978

Knopp M et al. *Quantitative Analysis of 92 12-Week Sub-elite Marathon Training Plans.* Sports Med Open. 2024;10:50. DOI: 10.1186/s40798-024-00717-5.

- Design/population: descriptive analysis of published sub-elite plans.
- Key use: practice description only; published recommendations are not effectiveness evidence.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38695978/

### PMID-31802956

Mølmen KS et al. *Block periodization of endurance training - a systematic review and meta-analysis.* Open Access J Sports Med. 2019;10:145-160. DOI: 10.2147/OAJSM.S180408.

- Design/population: mixed endurance-sport studies, generally small samples.
- Key use: possible small benefits for some aerobic/power outcomes; low directness for one running session.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31802956/

## Intensity domains and testing

### PMID-41931241

Anderson M et al. *The Measurement and Application of Critical Speed and D' in Running: A Scoping Review.* Sports Med. 2026;56(7):1637-1674. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-026-02410-x.

- Design/population: scoping review, 124 running studies.
- Key use: critical speed as the heavy-severe boundary; CS/D' as performance determinants and tools for individualized prescription; protocol/model sensitivity.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41931241/

### PMID-40134905

Lipková L et al. *Field-based tests for determining critical speed among runners and its practical application: a systematic review.* Front Sports Act Living. 2025;7:1520914. DOI: 10.3389/fspor.2025.1520914.

- Design/population: systematic review, 19 field-running studies.
- Key use: time trials and 3-minute all-out tests can estimate CS reliably under specified conditions; test setup matters.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40134905/

### PMID-39048094

Hunter B et al. *The Relationship Between the Moderate-Heavy Boundary and Critical Speed in Running.* Int J Sports Physiol Perform. 2024;19(9):963-972. DOI: 10.1123/ijspp.2024-0101.

- Design/population: systematic review, 26 studies and 527 participants.
- Key use: first-threshold estimates occur at a variable fraction of CS; useful group estimate, not an individual substitute for testing.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39048094/

### PMID-37462761

Kaufmann S et al. *Heart Rate Variability-Derived Thresholds for Exercise Intensity Prescription in Endurance Sports: A Systematic Review of Interrelations and Agreement with Different Ventilatory and Blood Lactate Thresholds.* Sports Med Open. 2023;9:59. DOI: 10.1186/s40798-023-00607-2.

- Design/population: 27 studies, 461 healthy participants; predominantly male endurance athletes.
- Key use: different threshold methods show individual limits of agreement; labels should not be treated as interchangeable.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37462761/

## Threshold, aerobic power, interval programming, and recovery

### PMID-38842374

Pereira HV et al. *Effects of high-intensity interval and moderate-intensity continuous training on anaerobic threshold of highly trained endurance athletes: systematic review and meta-analysis.* J Sports Med Phys Fitness. 2024;64(9):898-907. DOI: 10.23736/S0022-4707.24.15855-0.

- Design/population: three studies, 72 highly trained endurance athletes; high heterogeneity and low certainty.
- Key use: threshold outcome evidence is too limited to claim one universally superior format.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38842374/

### PMID-42237396

Schoenmakers P et al. *Time spent at or near VO2max during high-intensity interval training - a systematic review and meta-analysis.* BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil. 2026. DOI: 10.1186/s13102-026-01766-x.

- Design/population: 86 articles, 239 acute HIIT protocols; mixed modalities and training status.
- Key use: work bouts ≥2 minutes and greater cumulative duration generally accumulate more high-oxygen-uptake time; acute proxy, not guaranteed chronic superiority.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42237396/

### PMID-42482078

Held S et al. *Comparison of high-intensity interval training protocol designs on accumulated time ≥ 90% VO2max: a network meta-analysis.* BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil. 2026;18(1):336. DOI: 10.1186/s13102-026-01891-7.

- Design/population: network meta-analysis of 19 publications, 20 study units, and 240 healthy athletes; protocol nodes classified by work duration, work-rest structure, intensity pattern, and within-session progression.
- Key use: no HIIT structure differed significantly from even long intervals for accumulated time ≥90% VO2max; exploratory rankings were limited by substantial heterogeneity, significant inconsistency, and sparse nodes.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42482078/

### PMID-39835194

Fleckenstein D, Braunstein H, Walter N. *Faster intervals, faster recoveries - intensified short VO2max running intervals are inferior to traditional long intervals in terms of time spent above 90% VO2max.* Front Sports Act Living. 2024;6:1507957. DOI: 10.3389/fspor.2024.1507957.

- Design/population: acute randomized crossover, 12 highly trained middle-distance runners (7 male, 5 female).
- Key use: in the tested protocols, 4 × 3 minutes accumulated more time >90% VO2max than 24 × 30 seconds.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39835194/

### PMID-33826121

Rosenblat MA et al. *Programming Interval Training to Optimize Time-Trial Performance: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.* Sports Med. 2021;51(8):1687-1714. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-021-01457-2.

- Design/population: 29 studies, 67 groups, 491 adults; inactive through trained, mixed endurance modalities.
- Key use: training status, work duration, total work, weeks, and session count modify TT response; no universal optimal dose.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33826121/

### PMID-41740126

Xiao Y et al. *Effects of High-Intensity Interval Training Versus Sprint Interval Training on Factors Related to Endurance Performance: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.* J Strength Cond Res. 2026;40(3):344-355. DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0000000000005374.

- Design/population: 12 randomized trials, 460 participants; mixed training status/modalities.
- Key use: HIIT outperformed SIT for VO2max and MAP/MAV, but most outcome certainty was low or very low.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41740126/

### PMID-23539308

Buchheit M, Laursen PB. *High-intensity interval training, solutions to the programming puzzle: Part I.* Sports Med. 2013;43(5):313-338. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-013-0029-x.

- Design/population: programming review across endurance and athletic populations.
- Key use: HIIT response is created by multiple interacting variables rather than workout label alone.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23539308/

### PMID-23832851

Buchheit M, Laursen PB. *High-intensity interval training, solutions to the programming puzzle: Part II.* Sports Med. 2013;43(10):927-954. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-013-0066-5.

- Design/population: programming review.
- Key use: long-term programming, dose, and athlete characteristics; directness varies by protocol and sport.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23832851/

### PMID-38443585

Zouhal H et al. *Effects of Passive or Active Recovery Regimes Applied During Long-Term Interval Training on Physical Fitness in Healthy Trained and Untrained Individuals: A Systematic Review.* Sports Med Open. 2024;10:21. DOI: 10.1186/s40798-024-00673-0.

- Design/population: 24 studies, trained/untrained adults and some trained youth; heterogeneous protocols.
- Key use: both active and passive recovery can support adaptation; no consistent universal winner.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38443585/

### PMID-35262762

Sánchez-Otero T et al. *Active vs. passive recovery during an aerobic interval training session in well-trained runners.* Eur J Appl Physiol. 2022;122:1281-1291. DOI: 10.1007/s00421-022-04926-2.

- Design/population: acute crossover, 11 well-trained male runners, 4 × 2 minutes at maximal aerobic speed.
- Key use: similar time >90% VO2max; passive recovery reduced perceived load in this protocol.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35262762/

## Speed, economy, hills, and warm-up

### PMID-36165995

Hall AJ et al. *The Effects of Sprint Interval Training on Physical Performance: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.* J Strength Cond Res. 2023;37(2):457-481. DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0000000000004257.

- Design/population: 55 studies in healthy adults; heterogeneous SIT protocols.
- Key use: sprint/anaerobic qualities are trainable; possible small-study effects and limited directness to distance runners.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36165995/

### PMID-38041768

Thurlow F et al. *The Effects of Repeated-Sprint Training on Physical Fitness and Physiological Adaptation in Athletes: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.* Sports Med. 2024;54(4):953-974. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-023-01959-1.

- Design/population: 40 publications, 541 trained athletes in RST groups; substantial team-sport representation.
- Key use: speed, repeated-sprint ability, and VO2max can improve; low directness for continuous-distance runners.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38041768/

### PMID-42477971

Hutchinson M et al. *Number of Sprint Repetitions in a Sprint Interval Training Session Does Not Moderate Improvements in VO2max With Training: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.* Scand J Med Sci Sports. 2026;36(7):e70347. DOI: 10.1111/sms.70347.

- Design/population: SIT interventions in healthy adults.
- Key use: more sprint repetitions are not automatically more effective for VO2max.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42477971/

### PMID-38165636

Llanos-Lagos C et al. *Effect of Strength Training Programs in Middle- and Long-Distance Runners' Economy at Different Running Speeds: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis.* Sports Med. 2024;54(4):895-932. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-023-01978-y.

- Design/population: 31 studies, 652 middle-/long-distance runners; interventions 6-24 weeks.
- Key use: high-load, combined, and some plyometric programs can improve running economy; not evidence for a few drills.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38165636/

### PMID-29249083

Blagrove RC et al. *Effects of Strength Training on the Physiological Determinants of Middle- and Long-Distance Running Performance: A Systematic Review.* Sports Med. 2018;48(5):1117-1149. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-017-0835-7.

- Design/population: 24 controlled studies in experienced distance runners; interventions ≥4 weeks.
- Key use: running economy, 1.5-10 km TT, and sprint outcomes often improve; VO2max usually does not.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29249083/

### PMID-41766811

Feng Y et al. *High-Intensity Interval Training and Moderate-Intensity Continuous Training Affect Running Economy in Endurance Runners: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials.* J Hum Kinet. 2026;100:113-134. DOI: 10.5114/jhk/205427.

- Design/population: randomized studies in endurance runners; substantial heterogeneity.
- Key use: possible running-economy benefit from HIIT; certainty/directness lower than strength evidence.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41766811/

### PMID-23538293

Barnes KR et al. *Effects of different uphill interval-training programs on running economy and performance.* Int J Sports Physiol Perform. 2013;8(6):639-647. DOI: 10.1123/ijspp.8.6.639.

- Design/population: six-week randomized comparison, 20 well-trained runners.
- Key use: several hill formats improved 5-km performance on average; no single format clearly optimal.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23538293/

### PMID-36875023

Held S et al. *Increased oxygen uptake in well-trained runners during uphill high intensity running intervals: randomized crossover testing.* Front Physiol. 2023. DOI: 10.3389/fphys.2023.1117314.

- Design/population: acute crossover, 17 well-trained runners (8 female, 9 male), 4 × 5 minutes uphill versus near-flat.
- Key use: uphill running increased high-oxygen-uptake time in the tested protocol.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36875023/

### PMID-42536523

Tham SL et al. *Effects of uphill running on muscle strength, jumping performance, running time, and aerobic capacity outcomes in athletes and recreational adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis.* Medicine. 2026;105(31):e49977. DOI: 10.1097/MD.0000000000049977.

- Design/population: 13 randomized studies, 308 participants; small heterogeneous studies.
- Key use: promising individual effects but no consistent pooled superiority; low certainty.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42536523/

### PMID-19996770

Fradkin AJ et al. *Effects of warming-up on physical performance: a systematic review with meta-analysis.* J Strength Cond Res. 2010;24(1):140-148. DOI: 10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181c643a0.

- Design/population: 32 studies across sports and performance tests.
- Key use: most assessed performance criteria improved after an adequate warm-up; broad rather than running-specific.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19996770/

### PMID-35096248

Ullman ZJ et al. *Effects of Isometric Exercises versus Static Stretching in Warm-up Regimens for Running Sport Athletes: A Systematic Review.* Int J Exerc Sci. 2021;14(6):1204-1218.

- Design/population: nine running-sport studies; limited quality and heterogeneous outcomes.
- Key use: static stretching may impair some sprint/jump outcomes and does not show a clear running-economy advantage.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35096248/

### PMID-40442558

Warneke K et al. *Acute and Chronic Effects of Stretching on Running Economy: A Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis.* Sports Med Open. 2025;11:61. DOI: 10.1186/s40798-025-00859-0.

- Design/population: 15 acute studies, 181 healthy participants; low-certainty, heterogeneous protocols.
- Key use: no significant acute effect of static, dynamic, or PNF stretching on running economy; do not oversimplify stretching claims.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40442558/

### PMID-37644585

Li FY et al. *A systematic review and network meta-analysis of the effects of different warm-up methods on the acute effects of lower limb explosive strength.* BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil. 2023;15:106. DOI: 10.1186/s13102-023-00703-6.

- Design/population: 35 studies across athletic populations.
- Key use: dynamic warm-up tends to support sprint/jump outcomes; directness to endurance running is moderate/low.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37644585/

## Race demands

### PMID-11194103

Spencer MR, Gastin PB. *Energy system contribution during 200- to 1500-m running in highly trained athletes.* Med Sci Sports Exerc. 2001;33(1):157-162. DOI: 10.1097/00005768-200101000-00024.

- Design/population: 20 Australian national-standard athletes; event-specialist groups were small and male.
- Key use: 800 m and 1500 m require substantial aerobic and anaerobic contribution; no direct 1000 m percentage.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11194103/

### PMID-34021488

Haugen T et al. *Crossing the Golden Training Divide: The Science and Practice of Training World-Class 800- and 1500-m Runners.* Sports Med. 2021;51:1835-1854. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-021-01481-2.

- Design/population: scientific/practice review of world-class middle-distance runners.
- Key use: event demands and athlete profiles support combining aerobic power, speed, and speed endurance rather than one repeated session.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34021488/

### PMID-41900531

Thuany M et al. *A Systematic Review of the Factors Associated with Performance in Non-Elite Runners.* J Funct Morphol Kinesiol. 2026;11(1):124. DOI: 10.3390/jfmk11010124.

- Design/population: systematic review, 66 observational studies, race distances 5 km through ultramarathon.
- Key use: relevant predictors and terminology vary by race distance; associations are not intervention evidence.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41900531/

## Durability and marathon

### PMID-40150840

Hunter B et al. *Durability as an index of endurance exercise performance: Methodological considerations.* Exp Physiol. 2025;110(11):1612-1624. DOI: 10.1113/EP092120.

- Design/population: methodological review of endurance durability research.
- Key use: durability is resilience to deterioration during/after prolonged exercise; protocol, nutrition, environment, and training status affect measurement.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40150840/

### PMID-41128270

Hunter B, Muniz-Pumares D. *Durability of Parameters Associated With Endurance Running in Marathoners.* Eur J Sport Sci. 2025;25(11):e70073. DOI: 10.1002/ejsc.70073.

- Design/population: 18 2024 London Marathon participants, 11 male; mean finish 3:17; pre/post 90-minute run at the runner's fresh-state lactate-threshold speed.
- Key use: VO2peak and lactate-threshold speed deteriorated; smaller threshold-speed deterioration was associated with faster marathon performance.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41128270/

### PMID-39384626

Nuuttila OP et al. *Durability in recreational runners: effects of 90-min low-intensity exercise on the running speed at the lactate threshold.* Eur J Appl Physiol. 2025;125(3):697-705. DOI: 10.1007/s00421-024-05631-y.

- Design/population: 31 recreational runners, 15 female; 90 minutes at 90% of first-threshold speed.
- Key use: first-threshold speed fell about 5-6% in both sexes; supports fatigue-resistance as distinct from fresh-state markers.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39384626/

### PMID-40421635

Zanini M, Folland JP, Blagrove RC. *Test-Retest Reliability of Running Economy and Other Physiological Parameters During 90 min of Running in Well-Trained Male Endurance Runners.* Scand J Med Sci Sports. 2025;35(6):e70080. DOI: 10.1111/sms.70080.

- Design/population: 14 well-trained male marathoners; repeated 90-minute heavy-domain treadmill runs at 10% Δ between LT1 and LT2.
- Key use: running-economy durability can be measured reliably under controlled conditions; not an intervention proving how to improve it.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40421635/

### PMID-35511416

Smyth B et al. *Decoupling of Internal and External Workload During a Marathon: An Analysis of Durability in 82,303 Recreational Runners.* Sports Med. 2022;52(9):2283-2295. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-022-01680-5.

- Design/population: observational analysis, 82,303 marathoners including 13,125 female; internal load expressed as percentage of maximum heart rate and external load as speed relative to estimated critical speed.
- Key use: magnitude and onset of heart-rate/speed decoupling were associated with marathon performance; no causal training prescription.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35511416/

## Taper and environment

### PMID-17762369

Bosquet L et al. *Effects of tapering on performance: a meta-analysis.* Med Sci Sports Exerc. 2007;39(8):1358-1365. DOI: 10.1249/mss.0b013e31806010e0.

- Design/population: 27 endurance-sport studies.
- Key use: reducing volume while retaining intensity/frequency is a robust broad taper principle; exact timing remains event/program dependent.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17762369/

### PMID-37163550

Wang Z et al. *Effects of tapering on performance in endurance athletes: A systematic review and meta-analysis.* PLoS One. 2023;18(5):e0282838. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0282838.

- Design/population: 14 endurance-sport studies.
- Key use: TT/TTE improved; successful strategies generally reduced volume 41-60%, retained intensity/frequency, and lasted no more than 21 days.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37163550/

### PMID-25943653

Racinais S et al. *Consensus recommendations on training and competing in the heat.* Scand J Med Sci Sports. 2015;25 Suppl 1:6-19. DOI: 10.1111/sms.12467.

- Design/population: expert consensus integrating heat-exercise evidence.
- Key use: heat increases physiological strain and impairs endurance capacity; repeated heat exposure, hydration, cooling, and scheduling are relevant.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25943653/

### PMID-38051495

Brown HA et al. *Quantifying Exercise Heat Acclimatisation in Athletes and Military Personnel: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.* Sports Med. 2024;54(3):727-741. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-023-01972-4.

- Design/population: 18 articles from 12 studies; athletes and military personnel relocated to hotter climates.
- Key use: acclimatisation can reduce resting/exercising core temperature and heart rate; response depends on exposure conditions and duration.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38051495/

### PMID-34652333

Mantzios K et al. *Effects of Weather Parameters on Endurance Running Performance: Discipline-specific Analysis of 1258 Races.* Med Sci Sports Exerc. 2022;54(1):153-161. DOI: 10.1249/MSS.0000000000002769.

- Design/population: observational analysis of 1,258 elite races and 7,867 athletes across 3,000 m steeplechase through marathon/race walk.
- Key use: WBGT and temperature were associated with performance; fixed pace penalties for an Odyssey session are not directly established.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34652333/

### PMID-39762944

Solomon TPJ, Laye MJ. *The effect of post-exercise heat exposure (passive heat acclimation) on endurance exercise performance: a systematic review and meta-analysis.* BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil. 2025;17:4. DOI: 10.1186/s13102-024-01038-6.

- Design/population: healthy adults completing exercise training plus post-exercise sauna/hot-water exposure.
- Key use: passive heat exposure may complement acclimation; not an Odyssey Tuesday-session requirement.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39762944/

## Registry maintenance notes

- Keep source IDs stable.
- Add a source only after verifying its publication details and central result.
- When a topic claim relies on a small primary study, pair it with a review where possible and preserve the sample limitations.
- Do not reproduce abstracts or long quotations here.
- A source appearing in this registry does not mean every conclusion in the paper is adopted by Odyssey.
